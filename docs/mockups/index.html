Kamerton mockups

Interactive design mockups made while designing the app. Microphone input is simulated; the Reference tone buttons really play sound in the browser. Open a card to try it full size. Settings can be changed with URL parameters, e.g. ?theme=light&startInstrument=cello.

v1 — first design

Separate Listen and Reference modes, needle / strobe / LED bar displays, headstock or string chips.

v2 — one screen, reference tone built in

The Listen / Reference toggle is gone and the mic is always on. Reference controls are disabled while strings are detected automatically. The level meter shows dB, a peak tick and the noise gate marker.

v3 — layout B refined

Strings panel for every instrument, A4 next to the tuning menu, display style buttons under the tuner.

v4 — noise gate on the level meter

Adjust the noise gate while tuning. Plucks ring out and decay into the room noise; the tuner dims while the input is below the gate. Try dragging the orange marker, the arrow keys and the mouse wheel on it. Add &room=noisy for a louder room. All colors come from KDE color scheme roles: theme=dark, light (Breeze) or layan.

v5 — noise gate in its own panel

The noise gate leaves the tuner card and gets a panel of its own, with a “Noise gate” heading like Strings and Reference tone. The meter, Reset and Set from room noise work as in v4 E. Try &room=noisy and the three themes.

v6 — hardware-style needle

The v5 D layout with the needle drawn like studio hardware. A and C take every color from the KDE color scheme; B, the chosen one, uses KDE colors too, except for what KDE has no role for: the lamp-lit face, its printed ink and the bright In tune lamp. The needle has some weight and drops to its stop when there is no sound. gauge=flat is the v5 needle for comparison.

v7 — readouts and frames

v6 B with the glass and LED. The bezels around the meter take a dark KDE color instead of black, and the row under the meter is plain text: the frequency and the target on the left, the big note in the middle, the cents and what to do on the right.
