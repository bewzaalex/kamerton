Kamerton mockups

Interactive design mockups made while designing the app. Microphone input is simulated; the Reference tone buttons really play sound in the browser. Open a card to try it full size. Settings can be changed with URL parameters, e.g. ?theme=light&startInstrument=cello.

v1 — first design

Separate Listen and Reference modes, needle / strobe / LED bar displays, headstock or string chips.

v2 — one screen, reference tone built in

The Listen / Reference toggle is gone and the mic is always on. Reference controls are disabled while strings are detected automatically. The level meter shows dB, a peak tick and the noise gate marker.

v3 — layout B refined

Strings panel for every instrument, A4 next to the tuning menu, display style buttons under the tuner.

v4 — noise gate on the level meter

Adjust the noise gate while tuning. Plucks ring out and decay into the room noise; the tuner dims while the input is below the gate. Try dragging the orange marker, the arrow keys and the mouse wheel on it. Add &room=noisy for a louder room. All colors come from KDE color scheme roles: theme=dark, light (Breeze) or layan.

v5 — noise gate in its own panel

The noise gate leaves the tuner card and gets a panel of its own, with a “Noise gate” heading like Strings and Reference tone. The meter, Reset and Set from room noise work as in v4 E. Try &room=noisy and the three themes.

v6 — hardware-style needle

The v5 D layout with the needle drawn like studio hardware. A and C take every color from the KDE color scheme; B, the chosen one, uses KDE colors too, except for what KDE has no role for: the lamp-lit face, its printed ink and the bright In tune lamp. The needle has some weight and drops to its stop when there is no sound. gauge=flat is the v5 needle for comparison.

v7 — readouts and frames

v6 B with the glass and LED. The bezels around the meter take a dark KDE color instead of black, and the row under the meter is plain text: the frequency and the target on the left, the big note in the middle, the cents and what to do on the right.

v8 — window sizes

How the v7 layout adapts to the window. The Detector always comes first and its meter is as big as the window allows; Instrument comes second and Noise gate third. The page scrolls only when nothing else helps. Pick a size at the top, type one in, or drag the window’s bottom-right corner; the line at the top says what was folded. Click a panel heading to fold or unfold it by hand.

v9 — compact panels

Two states instead of folding step by step. When the window has room, every panel is shown in full. Otherwise the Detector stays and Instrument and Noise gate are replaced by small panels; the button with their name opens the full panel as a popup window, like a menu. It is placed on the screen, not inside the tuner window, so a tiny tuner in a corner next to a DAW still opens the full panel with the headstock. The popup closes on a click outside or Esc. Only a very small screen gets the string chips in the popup instead of the headstock. Drag the window by its title bar or its bottom-right corner.

v10 — Les Paul style headstock

v9 with a new headstock in outline C from v11 (traced from a straight-on photo of a Les Paul Custom and smoothed): the open-book top, a waist a third of the way down, shoulders below the lowest posts and a quick curve into the nut, black face with cream binding, a plain bell cover, chrome bushings and a short piece of fretboard; no branding. The tuner keys are wide chrome buttons with the note, string number and frequency; click one to pick the string. The whole headstock is one drawing that only scales as a whole, so its proportions never change with the window. It has its own colors, like the VU meter; the chosen string and the tuned badge use KDE colors. inlay=diamond adds a split-diamond inlay.

v11 — headstock outlines

Les Paul headstock outlines on their own, without the app, traced from straight-on photos: a waist about a third of the way down, shoulders below the lowest posts, then a quick curve into the nut. The chosen outline goes into the v10 app mockup.

v12 — colored panels (not used, the app stays with v10)

v10 with each panel in its own color, like the sections of Fender Studio’s effects: a colored title band with the heading in capitals and a colored border, or the border only. Colors: Fender-like teal, red and amber; the same toned down; or the KDE scheme’s accent, positive and neutral roles.
